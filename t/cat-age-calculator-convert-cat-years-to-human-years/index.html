How It Works

Cats age much faster than humans during the first two years of their lives. The standard calculation used by veterinarians assumes:

  • Year 1: Equals approximately 15 human years.
  • Year 2: Adds another 9 human years (making a 2-year-old cat roughly 24 in human years).
  • Year 3 and beyond: Each subsequent year adds approximately 4 human years.

Months are factored proportionally based on the rate of the current year.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Is the 1-to-7 ratio accurate for cats?

A: No. The old rule of thumb that one human year equals seven cat years is inaccurate. Cats reach maturity much faster in their first two years of life.

Q: When is a cat considered a senior?

A: Cats are generally considered senior citizens when they reach 11 years of age, which equates to roughly 60 human years.

Q: Do indoor cats live longer?

A: Yes, indoor cats typically have a longer lifespan than outdoor cats, which changes how their lifetime spans across human equivalent years.
